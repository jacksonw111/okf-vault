消息：三星电子正谋划逆向竞争策略，计划采购性价比更高的中国产DRAM存储芯片以降低手机生产成本，借国产厂商成本承压减产的窗口期，以低价机型突围中国市场。

韩国产业经济研究所研究员认为，“对中国产DRAM的需求预计将增加，苹果和三星电子也可能因成本压力而考虑采用中国DRAM。”

> 引用内容

> [EXCLUSIVE] SAMSUNG’S CONTRARIAN BET: USING “CHINESE CHIPS” TO BREAK INTO CHINA’S MOBILE MARKET
> 
> As “chipflation,” or rising component prices, shakes the global semiconductor market and sharply contracts China’s smartphone market, Samsung Electronics is seriously considering using competitively priced, low-cost Chinese DRAM to target the country’s mid- and low-end smartphone segment.
> 
> According to semiconductor industry sources on July 27, Samsung Electronics is exploring a contrarian strategy of significantly reducing smartphone manufacturing costs by adopting locally sourced semiconductors, including inexpensive Chinese mobile DRAM, while substantially increasing shipments of affordable smartphones in China.
> 
> The company aims to capitalize on a market opening created as local competitors cut smartphone shipments in response to rising component prices triggered by the artificial intelligence data-center boom.
> 
> The global mobile industry has already begun to feel the effects of chipflation since the beginning of this year. Major manufacturers, including Apple, Xiaomi, Oppo, and Vivo, have either raised the prices of new products or lowered their shipment targets by approximately 15% to 20% as they struggle with mounting component-cost pressures.
> 
> Apple raised the prices of MacBooks and iPads by about $100 in June, and the market widely expects the company to increase the prices of its next-generation iPhones scheduled to launch this fall.
> 
> The bigger problem lies with Chinese smartphone makers focused on the mid- and low-end market, which lack the exceptional brand power of Apple or Huawei. Companies such as Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, and Honor have difficulty passing higher component costs directly on to consumers. As a result, they are voluntarily reducing production under the judgment that selling more devices would only increase their losses.
> 
> Samsung Electronics sees this situation as a potential opportunity.
> 
> While major local manufacturers hesitate under cost pressure, Samsung is considering strategically adopting lower-priced Chinese DRAM to substantially reduce its device costs. By using the resulting price competitiveness to expand supplies of affordable models such as the Galaxy A series, the company could significantly increase its share of the Chinese smartphone market, which currently stands at only about 0.6%, while also expanding the overall revenue volume of its Mobile eXperience, or MX, division.
> 
> Expectations for the MX division’s financial performance have also fallen sharply among brokerage firms, as surging memory prices driven by the AI boom have significantly increased smartphone manufacturing costs.
> 
> Several major securities firms have recently lowered their full-year earnings estimates for Samsung Electronics’ MX and Networks division or suggested that the business may have swung to an operating loss in the second quarter.
> 
> Estimated second-quarter losses include KRW 200 billion from Hana Securities, KRW 584.1 billion from Samsung Securities, KRW 1 trillion from Eugene Investment & Securities, and KRW 800 billion from iM Securities.
> 
> Samsung Securities, in particular, previously projected that the division would generate KRW 3.41 trillion in operating profit this year, but it sharply revised that estimate to an operating loss of KRW 5.841 trillion, heightening concerns over the business.
> 
> Industry experts also view Samsung Electronics’ consideration of Chinese DRAM as both a realistic and potentially significant strategic option.
> 
> Shim Woo-jung, a research fellow responsible for home appliances and digital transformation at the Korea Institute for Industrial Economics and Trade, said, “Demand for Chinese-made DRAM is expected to increase, particularly among Chinese companies, and Apple and Samsung Electronics may also consider adopting Chinese DRAM because of cost pressures.”
> 
> He added, “With major Chinese smartphone manufacturers such as Xiaomi having significantly reduced production, Samsung Electronics could meaningfully increase its market share by expanding the supply of lower-priced smartphones such as the Galaxy A series.”
> 
> Shim also said, “Given that securities firms are estimating a possible second-quarter operating loss for Samsung Electronics’ MX division, the company is likely facing an increased sense of urgency.”
> 
> A Samsung Electronics official said the matter “could not be confirmed.”
